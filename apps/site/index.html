About wind on façades

The wind a façade has to resist depends on where the building is and what lies upwind. The basic wind speed comes from the UK map and the site altitude; it is then reduced or increased by direction, season, the distance to the sea and how much town the wind crosses on the way, and by the height of the building above its neighbours.

On the building itself the pressure is not uniform. The windward face is pushed, while the side walls are pulled hardest at their upwind corners, where the flow separates. Two buildings close together can funnel the wind between them and raise that suction further. A cladding system also sees the air pressure inside the building, or, for a rainscreen, only part of the outside pressure.

Summarised from BS EN 1991-1-4 clauses 4 and 7, the UK National Annex NA.2.5 to NA.2.27, and CWCT Part 2 clause 2.2.

  • −1.6zone A when the wind funnels between buildings, NA.2.27 note e)
  • 1.226kg/m³, the air density for the UK, NA.2.18
  • 800 Paminimum design wind pressure on a façade, CWCT Part 2 Table 2.1

Why Wind Loader

A façade wind calculation draws on four documents and two charts read by eye, and has to be repeated for every direction and elevation. The usual tools each leave a gap.

  • Spreadsheets

    Fast once set up, but chart readings and zone widths are typed in by hand, and the sheet is copied from job to job.

  • Structural software

    Good for the frame, but cladding zones, funnelling and the CWCT design pressures are usually left to you.

  • Hand calculations

    Clear and checkable, but slow to repeat for four directions, four elevations and several blocks.

  • Wind Loader

    The National Annex charts digitised, every value sourced, every zone drawn on the building, and a report laid out for a checker.

What it does

  • Peak velocity pressure

    Altitude, direction, season and probability factors, and exposure read from Figures NA.7 and NA.8 for each direction's shoreline and town distances.

  • Zones on every elevation

    Zones A to E for wind onto each face, reference heights from Figure 7.4, displacement height from Annex A.5 and the size factor from Table NA.3.

  • Funnelling

    Enter the gap to the neighbouring building and the NA.2.27 funnelling coefficients are applied where the gap and height rules say they should be.

  • Sealed and rainscreen

    Net pressure with internal pressure for glazing, or the CWCT reductions for ventilated and pressure-equalised rainscreens.

  • The building in 3D

    Zones coloured by net pressure on each face, a design suction envelope, and elevation mark-ups with the corner widths dimensioned.

  • A Word report

    Summary tables, design data, elevation drawings and an appendix of working for each block, ready to edit and issue.

Checked against real wind load reports

Given the same chart readings, the calculation chain reproduces the published results of two engineering wind load reports to 1 in 105. With its own digitised charts, Wind Loader agrees with the printed pressures within 2%. Expected results were computed by a separate script, outside the engine, and each case notes where the printed figures differ and why, such as a different air density.

About

Wind Loader is an independent tool for façade engineers, cladding contractors and designers. It applies its authors' reading of BS EN 1991-1-4 with the UK National Annex, PD 6688-1-4 and the CWCT Standard for systemised building envelopes.

It is not produced, reviewed or endorsed by BSI, CEN or CWCT. Every design it touches must still be checked and approved by a qualified structural engineer. If something looks wrong, please say so: corrections shape what comes next.
